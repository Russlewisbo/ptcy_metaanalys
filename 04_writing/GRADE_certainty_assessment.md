# GRADE Certainty-of-Evidence Assessment
### PTCy vs CNI+MTX/MMF (Comparison 1) — Six Primary Outcomes
*Prepared: 2026-05-27*

---

## Framework

All evidence derives from observational studies (predominantly retrospective cohort and
registry studies) with a single RCT contributing to the OS outcome (Curtis 2025,
ALLG BM12-CAST, k=1 of 35 studies). GRADE starting certainty for observational evidence
is **LOW**. Downgrades are applied per the standard five GRADE domains; upgrades for
observational evidence are applied only when strict criteria are met (large magnitude
[OR ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5], dose-response, or all plausible residual confounding would oppose
the observed effect).

---

## 1. Overall Survival (OS)

**k = 35 paired studies · N = 17,661 (PTCy 4,659 / control 13,002)**  
**M1: OR 0.77 [0.71–0.83], P(OR < 1) = 100% · τ = 0.59**  
**M2 (steroid-adjusted): OR 0.83 [0.75–0.92], P(OR < 1) = 100%**

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | 34 retrospective/registry + 1 RCT (Curtis 2025); start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Serious: predominantly retrospective; selection bias plausible (PTCy more often in younger/haploidentical recipients, potentially confounding OS benefit toward overestimation). M2 partial attenuation (0.77 → 0.83) confirms residual confounding present but does not abolish the effect. | **No downgrade** (effect survives adjustment; large registry studies mitigate individual-study bias) |
| **Inconsistency** | Serious: I² = 68%, τ = 0.59 | **−1** |
| **Indirectness** | Not serious: direct Comparison 1 populations, relevant endpoints | 0 |
| **Imprecision** | Not serious: N = 17,661, CrI 0.71–0.83 entirely below null, large posterior mass | 0 |
| **Publication bias** | Undetected: large registries (EBMT, CIBMTR) unlikely to suppress negative OS findings | 0 |
| **Upgrade: large effect** | Not met (OR = 0.77; threshold < 0.50) | 0 |
| **Upgrade: confounders oppose** | Not met: selection bias likely inflates OS benefit | 0 |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −1 (inconsistency)  
**⊕⊕◯◯ CERTAINTY: LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have low certainty that PTCy reduces overall mortality (OR 0.77 [0.71–0.83]). The large corpus (k=35, N=17,661) and consistent direction across study types provide some reassurance, but substantial between-study heterogeneity (I²=68%) and the absence of a completed RCT powered for OS limit confidence. The partial attenuation in M2 (0.77→0.83) indicates some steroid-mediated confounding but a probable direct mortality benefit remains.*

## 2. Non-Relapse Mortality (NRM)

**k = 15 paired studies · N = 2,081 (PTCy 1,219 / control 862)**  
**M1: OR 0.86 [0.70–1.08], P(OR < 1) = 90% · τ = 0.56**  
**M2 (steroid-adjusted): OR 1.03 [0.79–1.33], P(OR < 1) = 43%**

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | All retrospective/registry; start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Very serious: complete mediation in M2 (OR 0.86 → 1.03) demonstrates that the unadjusted NRM benefit is almost entirely explainable by differential steroid exposure — this is a formal causal confounding signal, not just a hypothesis. Residual confounding (selection, prophylaxis intensity) likely inflates the apparent M1 benefit. | **−1** |
| **Inconsistency** | Not serious: I² = 39%, τ = 0.56; Cochran Q p = 0.07; modest heterogeneity | 0 |
| **Indirectness** | Not serious | 0 |
| **Imprecision** | Serious: M1 CrI 0.70–1.08 crosses the null; M2 CrI 0.79–1.33 fully crosses; N = 2,081 is modest for a mortality endpoint. P(OR < 1) = 90% in M1 indicates uncertainty, not certainty, of benefit. | **−1** |
| **Publication bias** | Possible: small-study effects with k=15; NRM-negative studies less likely published | 0 (not formally detectable) |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −2 (risk of bias, imprecision)  
**⊕◯◯◯ CERTAINTY: VERY LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have very low certainty about the effect of PTCy on non-relapse mortality. The M1 estimate (OR 0.86) suggests probable benefit, but complete attenuation in the GVHD-adjusted model (M2 OR 1.03) strongly indicates that this benefit is mediated through reduced steroid exposure rather than a direct NRM effect. When GVHD prophylaxis load is held constant, no residual NRM benefit is detectable. Any clinical inference about PTCy reducing NRM should be framed as steroid-mediated and conditional on PTCy's aGVHD benefit.*

## 3. Acute GVHD Grade II–IV (aGVHD)

**k = 24 paired studies · N = 3,837 (PTCy 1,705 / control 1,721)**  
**M1: OR 0.63 [0.54–0.74], P(OR < 1) = 100% · τ = 0.66**

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | All retrospective/registry; start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Serious: all retrospective; aGVHD grading is centre-dependent and protocol-dependent; some studies may under-diagnose aGVHD in PTCy arms (expectation bias); cross-study definition heterogeneity (MAGIC vs IBMTR criteria). However, the effect is mechanistically primary and biologically expected. | 0 (serious but not downgraded given mechanistic plausibility and direction consistency) |
| **Inconsistency** | Serious: I² = 63%, τ = 0.66 (highest τ of any outcome); reflects genuine heterogeneity in comparator arm GVHD risk across different donor types and conditioning regimens | **−1** |
| **Indirectness** | Not serious | 0 |
| **Imprecision** | Not serious: CrI 0.54–0.74 does not include 1; large N = 3,837 | 0 |
| **Publication bias** | Not seriously suspected: large registry-based studies with comprehensive reporting | 0 |
| **Upgrade: large effect** | Borderline: OR = 0.63 approaches but does not cross the < 0.50 threshold | 0 |
| **Upgrade: confounders oppose** | Partially met: misclassification of GVHD in PTCy arms (if any) would bias toward null, not away. A conservative estimate is likely. | +1 |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −1 + 1 = 0  
**⊕⊕◯◯ CERTAINTY: LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have low certainty that PTCy reduces aGVHD grade II–IV (OR 0.63 [0.54–0.74]). The finding is mechanistically central to the PTCy rationale, consistent across 24 studies including large registries, and the tight CrI indicates reliable signal despite heterogeneity. The dominant remaining uncertainty is whether aGVHD under-reporting in PTCy arms explains part of the apparent benefit — however, if anything this would mean our estimate is conservative. This is the outcome with the strongest mechanistic prior and arguably the most reliable signal in the corpus.*

## 4. CMV Any-Reactivation

**k = 18 paired studies · N = 3,357 (PTCy 1,039 / control 2,318)**  
**M1: OR 1.41 [1.20–1.65], P(OR > 1) = 100% · τ = 0.57**  
**M2 (steroid-adjusted): OR 1.43 [1.18–1.73] (no attenuation)**  
**M3 (aGVHD-adjusted): OR 1.52 [1.25–1.87] (no attenuation)**  
**Sensitivity: post-2020 (k=13): OR 1.80 [1.49–2.18]; haplo-only (k=11): OR 1.70 [1.39–2.10]**

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | All retrospective; start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Serious: CMV monitoring intensity varies widely across centres; more intensive surveillance in PTCy arms (due to awareness of increased risk) could inflate ascertainment of reactivation events. However, ascertainment bias would tend to overstate the PTCy harm, suggesting the true effect may be somewhat smaller. | 0 (serious but not downgraded; ascertainment direction noted in text) |
| **Inconsistency** | Serious: I² = 69%, τ = 0.57; driven by geographic seroprevalence variation and prophylaxis era effects (see RE investigation) | **−1** |
| **Indirectness** | Not serious | 0 |
| **Imprecision** | Not serious: CrI 1.20–1.65 does not include 1; N = 3,357 | 0 |
| **Publication bias** | Not strongly suspected: large registries included; harm outcomes less subject to publication bias than benefit outcomes | 0 |
| **Upgrade: robustness** | Direction consistent across all 4 pre-specified sensitivity models (M1 1.41, M2 1.43, M3 1.52, haplo 1.70, post-2020 1.80). Mediation analyses (M2, M3) confirm the signal is **steroid-independent**, removing the primary confounding explanation. | +1 |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −1 + 1 = 0  
**⊕⊕◯◯ CERTAINTY: LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have low certainty that PTCy increases CMV any-reactivation risk (OR 1.41 [1.20–1.65]). The finding is consistent across 18 observational studies and robust across all pre-specified sensitivity analyses. Critically, mediation analyses demonstrate this risk is independent of GVHD-mediated steroid exposure (M2 and M3 show no attenuation), pointing to direct T-cell reconstitution impairment. The frequentist estimate was non-significant (OR 1.22 [0.87–1.71]) due to insufficient power in the paired-study analysis; the Bayesian approach, which appropriately uses all single-arm data, provides higher precision.*

## 5. Bloodstream Infection (BSI, any pathogen)

**k = 6 paired studies · N = 1,319 (PTCy 723 / control 596)**  
**M1: OR 2.39 [1.85–3.10], P(OR > 1) = 100%, P(OR > 2) = 91% · τ = 0.64**  
**Frequentist: OR 2.45 [1.86–3.21], I² = 0%**  
*(13 of 19 candidate studies had NR event counts and were excluded)*

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | All retrospective; start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Very serious: 7 of 13 initially eligible paired studies had NR (not reported) BSI event counts and were excluded. This attrition is non-random — studies that did not specifically focus on infectious complications are most likely to report BSI as NR. Residual 6 studies may over-represent high-BSI centres. | **−1** |
| **Inconsistency** | Not serious: I² = 0%, consistent direction across all 6 studies | 0 |
| **Indirectness** | Mild concern: BSI definitions varied (positive blood culture vs. Sepsis-3 criteria); prophylaxis practices differ substantially. Only 5 of 6 studies used D+100 or D+180 timepoints. | 0 (not formally downgraded) |
| **Imprecision** | Serious: k = 6 provides limited ability to detect publication bias or correct for confounding. Although CrI 1.85–3.10 is entirely above null, the wide Bayesian τ = 0.64 reflects prior-driven uncertainty at small k. | **−1** |
| **Publication bias** | Suspected: only 6 studies; small-k bodies of evidence are disproportionately positive | 0 (not formally assessable) |
| **Upgrade: large magnitude** | Met: OR 2.39, P(OR > 2) = 91%; threshold of ≥ 2 is met, suggesting a large clinical effect if real | **+1** |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −2 + 1 = −1  
**⊕◯◯◯ CERTAINTY: VERY LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have very low certainty about the effect of PTCy on BSI risk. The available 6-study corpus shows a consistent, large OR of 2.39 [1.85–3.10] — but 7 additional studies with NR event counts were excluded, creating substantial risk that the true corpus average is more moderate. The absence of heterogeneity among included studies (I²=0%) and the large point estimate are notable, but k=6 is insufficient to draw firm conclusions. BSI prevention strategies — particularly early antibacterial prophylaxis optimisation — may be warranted pending larger dedicated analyses.*

## 6. Invasive Fungal Infection (IFI, any)

**k = 8 paired studies · N = 3,816 (PTCy 943 / control 2,873)**  
**M1: OR 0.73 [0.53–1.00], P(OR < 1) = 97.5% · τ = 1.05**  
**LOO excl. Yanada 2026: OR 0.75 [0.54–1.02], P(OR < 1) = 96.5%, τ = 0.38**

| Domain | Assessment | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Study design | All retrospective; start LOW | — |
| **Risk of bias** | Serious: IFI diagnosis requires bronchoscopy/biopsy (EORTC criteria) or is investigator-reported; PTCy arms may be under-investigated for IFI if clinical suspicion is lower. Two distinct definition frameworks used (EORTC 7 studies, investigator-reported 1 study). | 0 (effect direction — possible IFI under-diagnosis in PTCy — would bias toward apparently protective PTCy; noted in text) |
| **Inconsistency** | Very serious: I² = 82%, τ = 1.05. Leave-one-out analysis demonstrates that virtually all heterogeneity (τ: 1.05 → 0.38) is attributable to a single structural-zero study (Yanada 2026: 0 IFI events in 241 PTCy patients, consistent with universal mold-active prophylaxis). After excluding Yanada, τ collapses to 0.38 but k drops to 7 and the OR shifts only marginally (0.73 → 0.75). | **−2** (very serious) |
| **Indirectness** | Serious: antifungal prophylaxis regimens were heterogeneous across studies and almost certainly non-randomly distributed between arms; letermovir-era studies (2022+) differ qualitatively from earlier studies for IFI risk. | **−1** |
| **Imprecision** | Serious: CrI 0.53–1.00 just touches the null; LOO excl. Yanada CrI 0.54–1.02 crosses the null | **−1** |
| **Publication bias** | Possible: k=8; studies showing IFI benefit of PTCy more likely reported | 0 |

**Starting certainty:** LOW  
**Net adjustment:** −4 → floor at VERY LOW  
**⊕◯◯◯ CERTAINTY: VERY LOW**

> *Interpretation: We have very low certainty about the effect of PTCy on IFI risk. The posterior probability P(OR < 1) = 97.5% suggests a probable protective signal, but this must be interpreted cautiously: the entire heterogeneity structure is driven by a single zero-event outlier (Yanada 2026), antifungal prophylaxis practices are highly heterogeneous across studies, and the CrI just touches the null. The most defensible clinical inference is that PTCy does not appear to increase IFI risk, and may reduce it in centres with aggressive antifungal prophylaxis protocols — but this is insufficient to recommend changing prophylaxis practices based on current evidence.*

## GRADE Narrative Summary

Across all six outcomes, certainty of evidence does not exceed LOW — a predictable
consequence of the evidence base being almost entirely observational. No RCT with
numeric Comparison 1 data exists for aGVHD, NRM, CMV, BSI, or IFI, and the single
contributing RCT (Curtis 2025 for OS) is one study among 35.

Despite low certainty, several observations are methodologically notable:

1. **Consistent direction.** All six outcomes show directional signals aligning with
   the proposed biological mechanisms (PTCy reduces GVHD and immune toxicity; PTCy
   increases viral and bacterial infection risk). GRADE does not formally weight
   directional coherence as an upgrade criterion, but it substantially strengthens
   biological plausibility.

2. **Mediation analysis reduces confounding uncertainty.** The M2 models for NRM and
   OS provide a novel quality-of-evidence lever not available in standard GRADE
   assessments: the ability to test whether a confounder (steroid exposure) accounts
   for the observed effect. The complete mediation of NRM (OR 0.86→1.03) actually
   *increases* certainty that the unadjusted NRM benefit is confounded, while the
   persistence of OS and CMV effects despite M2 adjustment *strengthens* the case
   for those signals.

3. **Clinical decision threshold.** For the primary clinical question — should PTCy
   be preferred over CNI+MTX/MMF — the OS evidence (LOW certainty, OR 0.77) together
   with the aGVHD evidence (LOW certainty, OR 0.63) is likely sufficient to inform
   guideline recommendations, particularly in haploidentical settings. The CMV and
   BSI risk signals (LOW and VERY LOW certainty, respectively) should be incorporated
   into clinical monitoring and prophylaxis guidance, but at current certainty levels
   are insufficient to recommend against PTCy.
