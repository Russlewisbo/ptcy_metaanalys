# What This Study Adds

## Existing Evidence

Several systematic reviews and meta-analyses have examined outcomes after PTCy-based GVHD prophylaxis (Table 1). However, none has simultaneously evaluated infection and transplant outcomes across multiple comparator contexts, and none has proposed a unifying mechanistic framework linking GVHD suppression to infection risk.

| Study | Year | Comparison | k | Patients | Infection outcomes | Limitations |

## What the Present Study Adds

### 1. Scale

This is, to our knowledge, the largest systematic review of PTCy in allo-HCT, encompassing 251 studies (14 RCTs), 525 study arms, and 3,604 outcome observations. The largest prior comparative meta-analysis (Li et al. 2025) included 9 studies and 1,674 patients; the largest prior meta-analysis of any design (Arcuri et al. 2019) included 20 studies. The present corpus is an order of magnitude larger and spans all donor types, conditioning regimens, and geographic regions.

### 2. Infection as a primary endpoint in a comparative framework

Prior meta-analyses have treated infection outcomes as secondary or have been restricted to a single infection type. This is the first meta-analysis to systematically pool comparative data on CMV reactivation (k=22 for C1, k=13 for C2), bloodstream infections (k=6), and invasive fungal infections (k=6) alongside GVHD, NRM, and OS within the same analytic framework. The simultaneous analysis of benefit (GVHD, NRM, OS) and harm (CMV, BSI) outcomes enables mechanistic interpretation that is impossible in single-outcome reviews.

### 3. Dual-comparator design and the T-cell depletion hierarchy

All prior meta-analyses have examined a single comparison — typically haplo-PTCy vs. matched donor, or PTCy vs. ATG. This study's pre-specified dual-comparator design (C1: PTCy vs CNI+MTX/MMF; C2: PTCy vs ATG) reveals that the direction of PTCy's effect on CMV reverses depending on the comparator's own T-cell depletion depth:

- **C1 (vs CNI+MTX):** PTCy increases CMV risk (OR 1.26 [1.07–1.47])
- **C2 (vs ATG):** PTCy shows a directionally protective, null effect (OR 0.92 [0.73–1.15])

This pattern is internally replicated by two independent analyses: (a) the C2 expanded-arm sensitivity, where adding ATG to the PTCy arm converges CMV toward null; and (b) the C1 subgroup stratified by comparator-arm ATG use, where the CMV excess vanishes when the comparator also uses ATG (OR 1.05 vs 1.25). The resulting T-cell depletion hierarchy framework — CNI+MTX < PTCy < PTCy+ATG ≈ ATG — reframes PTCy-associated infection risk as a relative rather than intrinsic effect.

No prior meta-analysis has identified this pattern or proposed a mechanistic explanation for the apparently contradictory infection findings in the PTCy literature.

### 4. Bayesian mediation analysis of the GVHD–steroid–outcome pathway

Using Bayesian hierarchical models with steroid exposure as a mediator, this study shows that PTCy's NRM benefit is largely steroid-mediated (i.e., attributable to reduced GVHD and consequent steroid avoidance), while the OS benefit is only partially mediated — suggesting an additional, GVHD-independent survival contribution. Prior meta-analyses have reported pooled hazard ratios or risk ratios for OS and NRM without causal pathway decomposition.

### 5. Identification of haploidentical donor proportion as an effect modifier

Meta-regression identified haplo donor proportion as a significant moderator of both aGVHD (C1 β = +0.005/%, p = 0.049; C2 β = +0.013/%, p = 0.034) and OS (C1 β = +0.005/%, p = 0.003) treatment effects. PTCy's advantages over both comparators attenuate as haplo proportion increases, reaching null at ~100% haplo. This finding has direct implications for interpreting prior meta-analyses that did not account for donor-type composition.

### 6. Publication bias assessment across all outcomes

Application of Robust Bayesian Model-Averaging (RoBMA) to all 9 pooled outcomes detected strong evidence of publication bias for C1 OS and aGVHD and moderate evidence for C2 OS and aGVHD, with bias-adjusted effects collapsing toward the null. No prior PTCy meta-analysis has applied model-averaged publication bias correction.

## Summary Box

**What is already known on this topic:**
- PTCy is effective GVHD prophylaxis across donor types, supported by the BMT CTN 1703 RCT
- PTCy has been associated with increased CMV reactivation and bacterial infections in registry studies
- Existing meta-analyses (k = 4–20 studies) suggest comparable or lower CMV risk with PTCy vs ATG, but data are sparse and restricted to single comparisons

**What this study adds:**
- The largest systematic review of PTCy in allo-HCT (251 studies, 525 arms, 3,604 outcomes)
- The first comparative meta-analysis to simultaneously pool CMV, BSI, and IFI alongside GVHD, NRM, and OS
- A T-cell depletion hierarchy framework explaining why PTCy increases infection vs CNI-based regimens but not vs ATG — with internal replication across three independent analyses
- Evidence that PTCy's infection costs are a general consequence of T-cell–depleting prophylaxis, not a PTCy-specific limitation
- Identification of haplo donor proportion as a significant effect modifier that attenuates PTCy's advantages at high haplo fractions
- Bayesian mediation analysis showing steroid-mediated NRM benefit but only partial mediation of OS
