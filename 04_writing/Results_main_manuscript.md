## Search results and study characteristics

The database search identified 4,145 records (EMBASE 2,603, PubMed 1,061, SCOPUS 481),
of which 1,220 were duplicates (figure 1). After screening 2,925 titles and abstracts,
517 articles were sought for full-text review; 52 could not be retrieved or were not in
English. Of the 465 full-text articles assessed, 214 were excluded (appendix p XX),
leaving 251 studies for the systematic review. Of these, 117 studies with extractable
event counts contributed to the meta-analysis across three pre-specified comparisons:
PTCy versus CNI+MTX/MMF (Comparison 1 [C1]; 83 studies), PTCy versus ATG-based
prophylaxis (Comparison 2 [C2]; 42 studies), and within-PTCy regimen variants
(Comparison 3 [C3]; 13 studies).

The 225 comparative studies encompassed 520 arms and 177,758 patients (table 1). Most
were retrospective cohorts (132 [59%]) or registry analyses (62 [28%]); 14 (6%) were
randomised controlled trials. Median publication year was 2023 (IQR 2021–24), with
enrolment starting from a median of 2013 (IQR 2010–15) and median follow-up of 26 months
(IQR 20–36). PTCy arms were predominantly haploidentical donor transplants (127 [47%])
whereas comparator arms were predominantly matched unrelated (94 [38%]) or matched
sibling (54 [22%]) donor transplants.

## Risk of bias

Of 227 observational studies assessed with ROBINS-I, 150 (66%) were judged to be at
moderate risk of bias and 71 (31%) at serious risk, primarily due to confounding by donor
type and conditioning intensity (appendix p XX). Of 14 RCTs assessed with RoB 2,
11 had some concerns (predominantly due to unblinded outcome assessment) and one was
at low risk of bias.

## Comparison 1: PTCy versus CNI+MTX/MMF

### Survival outcomes

PTCy was associated with improved overall survival (OS; k=40, OR 0·79 [95% CrI
0·73–0·85]; P(OR<1)=100%; figure 2A). Adjusting for arm-level steroid exposure
(M2 model) attenuated the estimate to OR 0·86 [0·77–0·96], indicating partial
GVHD-mediated confounding but a residual direct benefit. Non-relapse mortality (NRM;
k=12) showed a non-significant trend toward benefit (OR 0·88 [0·66–1·18]; P(OR<1)=87%).
The M2 NRM estimate (OR 0·90 [0·59–1·36]) was indeterminate regarding steroid mediation.
Relapse-related mortality was reduced (k=38, OR 0·84 [0·76–0·93]; P(OR<1)>99%), with no
attenuation after steroid adjustment, consistent with preserved graft-versus-leukaemia
activity.

### GVHD outcomes

PTCy substantially reduced acute GVHD grade II–IV (k=28, OR 0·67 [0·59–0·78];
P(OR<1)=100%; figure 2B) and moderate-to-severe chronic GVHD (k=21, OR 0·33
[0·29–0·36]; P(OR<1)=100%) — the largest treatment effect in the analysis.

### Infection outcomes

CMV reactivation was increased with PTCy (k=22, OR 1·26 [1·07–1·47]; P(OR>1)>99%;
figure 2C). This effect was steroid-independent (M2 OR 1·25 [1·01–1·54]) and was
strengthened in sensitivity analyses restricted to post-2020 studies (k=17, OR 1·53
[1·28–1·85]) and haploidentical-dominant cohorts (k=12, OR 1·38 [1·12–1·71]).
Bloodstream infection risk was elevated (k=6, OR 1·87 [1·33–2·62]), though the
frequentist estimate was non-significant (OR 1·29 [0·61–2·73]) and heterogeneity was
substantial (I²=71%). BK virus reactivation showed the largest infection effect (k=11,
OR 2·48 [1·82–3·38]). Conversely, invasive fungal infection was reduced (k=6, OR 0·43
[0·29–0·63]), though extreme heterogeneity (τ=1·64) warrants caution. Infection-related
mortality was modestly elevated (k=16, OR 1·35 [1·16–1·57]) in the Bayesian model, though
the frequentist estimate was null (OR 1·01 [0·70–1·45]).

## Comparison 2: PTCy versus ATG-based prophylaxis

PTCy was associated with improved OS versus ATG (k=10, OR 0·81 [0·74–0·90];
P(OR<1)=100%; figure 3A), with negligible steroid-mediated attenuation (M2 OR 0·82
[0·71–0·94]). Acute GVHD grade II–IV was also reduced (k=9, OR 0·58 [0·44–0·77];
P(OR<1)>99%). NRM could not be assessed (k=2, below modelling threshold). CMV
reactivation was not significantly different between PTCy and ATG (k=13, OR 0·92
[0·73–1·15]), contrasting with the elevated risk seen versus CNI+MTX/MMF in C1. This
pattern — PTCy increasing CMV risk versus less immunosuppressive comparators but not
versus ATG — is consistent with CMV reactivation tracking the depth of T-cell depletion
across prophylaxis platforms. BK virus risk was similarly elevated versus ATG (k=3,
OR 2·44 [1·40–4·26]), supporting a comparator-independent urothelial mechanism.
Infection-related mortality was not increased versus ATG (k=7, OR 0·90 [0·74–1·09]).

## Comparison 3: within-PTCy regimen variants

No significant differences were detected between PTCy-based regimen variants for OS
(OR 1·11), NRM (OR 0·96), acute GVHD (OR 1·00), or chronic GVHD (OR 0·67); all
credible intervals crossed the null (k=4–7 per outcome).

## Sensitivity analyses and certainty of evidence

Leave-one-out analysis for the most heterogeneous outcome (IFI, τ=1·64) identified
Haebe 2023 as the dominant heterogeneity contributor (τ dropped from 1·11 to 0·82 on
exclusion). Frequentist random-effects estimates were directionally concordant with
Bayesian results for all outcomes, though framework-specific differences were notable for
BSI (Bayesian OR 1·87 vs frequentist OR 1·29) and IFI (Bayesian OR 0·43 vs frequentist
OR 0·60 [0·21–1·74]).

GRADE certainty of evidence was LOW for C1 OS, C1 aGVHD, C1 CMV, C2 OS, and C2 aGVHD,
and VERY LOW for C1 NRM, C1 BSI, C1 IFI, and C2 CMV (table 2). No outcome achieved
MODERATE or HIGH certainty, reflecting the predominantly observational evidence base.
The C1 CMV robustness upgrade was supported by consistent direction across 22 studies,
steroid independence, and two sensitivity analyses (post-2020 and haploidentical subsets).
