Comparison 1: PTCy vs CNI+MTX/MMF

Comparison 1 evaluates PTCy against calcineurin inhibitor–based prophylaxis (CNI+MTX or CNI+MMF) — the most common alternative platform. This comparison draws on the largest evidence base in the meta-analysis.

The pre-Block-9 M1→M2 shift (OR 0.86 → 1.02) was the strongest mediation finding. After arm reclassification and expanded data, the shift is now minimal (0.88 → 0.90) with wide CrIs. The steroid mediation hypothesis for NRM remains plausible but is no longer clearly supported by the Bayesian models alone.

NotecGVHD: The Strongest PTCy Effect

Chronic GVHD is the most profoundly affected outcome in the entire analysis. The moderate-severe cGVHD OR of 0.33 is substantially larger than the aGVHD effect (OR 0.67), consistent with PTCy’s mechanism: alloreactive T-cell depletion during the early post-transplant window has an outsized impact on the chronic alloimmune processes that drive cGVHD. This has direct implications for long-term quality of life, immunosuppression duration, and late NRM.

ImportantBK Virus: Dual Mechanism

Unlike CMV and BSI (which operate through T-cell lymphodepletion alone), BK virus reactivation has a dual mechanism: (1) direct urothelial toxicity from cyclophosphamide metabolites (acrolein), and (2) impaired BK-specific immune reconstitution. This explains why the BK effect (OR 2.48) is larger than CMV (OR 1.26) and BSI (OR 1.87).

Bayesian M1: OR 1.35 (95% CrI 1.16–1.57), P(OR > 1) = 100%, k = 16, τ = 0.84. The Bayesian binomial model finds elevated infection-related mortality with PTCy.

Frequentist RE: OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.70–1.45), I² = 70%. The frequentist random-effects model finds a null effect.

WarningBayesian–Frequentist Divergence

The Bayesian and frequentist models disagree on C1 IRM. The divergence is driven by two large CIBMTR registry studies (Battipaglia 2019: IRM 41% vs 25%; Mulroney 2021: IRM 35% vs 26%) that together contribute >2,400 patients. The Bayesian binomial model weights these heavily (they carry the most binomial information); the frequentist RE model equalizes study contributions through the random-effects structure, and smaller studies showing protective effects (Baron 2024, Montoro 2021, Tischer 2015) partially offset the CIBMTR signal.

Interpretation: The truth likely lies between these estimates. PTCy may modestly increase infection-related mortality (consistent with the Bayesian estimate), but the effect is uncertain and substantially smaller than the infection rate increases (CMV +26%, BSI +87%, BK +148%). The key message remains that the infection rate excess translates to a much smaller — and possibly null — mortality excess.

**Bayesian M1:** OR 1.35 (95% CrI 1.16–1.57), P(OR > 1) = 100%, k = 16, τ = 0.84. The Bayesian binomial model finds elevated infection-related mortality with PTCy.

**Frequentist RE:** OR 1.01 (95% CI 0.70–1.45), I² = 70%. The frequentist random-effects model finds a null effect.

::: {.callout-warning}
## Bayesian–Frequentist Divergence
The Bayesian and frequentist models **disagree** on C1 IRM. The divergence is driven by two large CIBMTR registry studies (Battipaglia 2019: IRM 41% vs 25%; Mulroney 2021: IRM 35% vs 26%) that together contribute >2,400 patients. The Bayesian binomial model weights these heavily (they carry the most binomial information); the frequentist RE model equalizes study contributions through the random-effects structure, and smaller studies showing protective effects (Baron 2024, Montoro 2021, Tischer 2015) partially offset the CIBMTR signal.

**Interpretation:** The truth likely lies between these estimates. PTCy may modestly increase infection-related mortality (consistent with the Bayesian estimate), but the effect is uncertain and substantially smaller than the infection *rate* increases (CMV +26%, BSI +87%, BK +148%). The key message remains that the infection rate excess translates to a much smaller — and possibly null — mortality excess.
:::
