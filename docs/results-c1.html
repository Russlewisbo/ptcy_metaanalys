Comparison 1 evaluates PTCy against calcineurin inhibitor–based prophylaxis (CNI+MTX or CNI+MMF) — the most common alternative platform. This comparison draws on the largest evidence base in the meta-analysis.

Overall Survival

  M1 = post_os$b_ptcy_binary,
  M2 = post_m2_os[[ptcy_col(post_m2_os)]]

  pivot_longer(everything(), names_to = "Model", values_to = "log_or") |>

  labs(x = "log-OR (PTCy vs CNI+MTX/MMF)", y = "Posterior density",
       title = "Overall Survival — Posterior Distributions") +
  theme_jama()

M1 (Direct effect): OR 0.79 (95% CrI 0.73–0.85), P(OR < 1) = 100%, τ = 0.61. The posterior is entirely below the null, indicating a consistent mortality reduction with PTCy.

M2 (Steroid-adjusted): Adjusting for arm-level systemic steroid exposure attenuated the OR from 0.79 to 0.86 (95% CrI 0.77–0.96), a 36% reduction in the log-OR. The residual benefit after adjustment indicates GVHD mediation does not fully explain the OS advantage.

M1: OR 0.88 (95% CrI 0.66–1.18), P(OR < 1) = 83%, τ = 0.72. Directionally favourable but CrI crosses null; k decreased from 15 (pre-Block-9) to 12 after arm reclassification.

M2: OR 0.90 (95% CrI 0.59–1.36), P(OR < 1) = 60%. The M1→M2 shift is modest (0.88 → 0.90), with wide CrIs overlapping substantially. Unlike the pre-Block-9 analysis (where M2 crossed null at 1.02, suggesting complete mediation), the current data are inconclusive regarding steroid mediation of NRM — the point estimates barely differ, and the wide intervals accommodate both full mediation and no mediation.

NoteNRM Mediation — Revised

The pre-Block-9 M1→M2 shift (OR 0.86 → 1.02) was the strongest mediation finding. After arm reclassification and expanded data, the shift is now minimal (0.88 → 0.90) with wide CrIs. The steroid mediation hypothesis for NRM remains plausible but is no longer clearly supported by the Bayesian models alone.

M1: OR 0.84 (95% CrI 0.76–0.93), P(OR < 1) > 99%, k = 38. PTCy is associated with reduced relapse-related mortality, consistent with preserved graft-versus-leukaemia (GVL) activity. This is the first meta-analytic estimate of this outcome.

M2 (steroid-adjusted): OR 0.83 (95% CrI 0.73–0.93). This contrasts with OS (partially mediated) and NRM (uncertain mediation), and helps decompose the residual OS benefit that survives steroid adjustment.

NoteRRM Completes the Causal Picture

The RRM finding resolves the question of what drives the residual OS benefit after steroid adjustment. OS M2 retains ~64% of the M1 log-OR; RRM retains ~100%. The portion of the OS advantage not explained by GVHD/steroid reduction is likely attributable to preserved anti-leukaemia immunity.

M1: OR 2.48 (95% CrI 1.82–3.38), P(OR > 1) > 99%, k = 11, τ = 1.06. The largest infection-outcome effect in the analysis. BK virus reactivation (haemorrhagic cystitis) is substantially more common with PTCy, consistent with cyclophosphamide-induced urothelial damage compounded by impaired BK-specific T-cell surveillance.

ImportantBK Virus: Dual Mechanism

Unlike CMV and BSI (which operate through T-cell lymphodepletion alone), BK virus reactivation has a dual mechanism: (1) direct urothelial toxicity from cyclophosphamide metabolites (acrolein), and (2) impaired BK-specific immune reconstitution. This explains why the BK effect (OR 2.48) is larger than CMV (OR 1.26) and BSI (OR 1.87).
