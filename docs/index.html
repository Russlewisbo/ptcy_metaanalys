Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of PTCy vs alternative GVHD prophylaxis platforms

Published

June 1, 2026

Abstract

Background. Post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy) has become widely adopted for GVHD prophylaxis in allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation, yet comprehensive quantitative evidence synthesis across survival, GVHD, and infection outcomes — and across comparator regimens — has been lacking.

Methods. We conducted a Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of 251 studies (14 RCTs) comparing PTCy to (1) CNI+MTX/MMF-based regimens (Comparison 1), (2) ATG-based regimens (Comparison 2), and (3) within-PTCy regimen variants (Comparison 3). Ten outcomes were analysed: OS, NRM, relapse-related mortality (RRM), acute GVHD grade II–IV, chronic GVHD moderate-severe (NIH), CMV reactivation, BK virus, BSI, IFI, and infection-related mortality (IRM). Steroid-mediation models (M2) tested whether GVHD-driven steroid exposure confounded observed effects.

Results. PTCy was associated with improved OS (C1: OR 0.79 [0.73–0.85]; C2: OR 0.82 [0.71–0.94]), reduced aGVHD (C1: OR 0.67; C2: OR 0.58), and a substantial reduction in moderate-severe chronic GVHD (C1: OR 0.33 — the largest treatment effect). RRM was reduced in both comparisons (C1: OR 0.83; C2: OR 0.79), supporting preserved GVL activity. CMV reactivation was increased vs CNI+MTX/MMF (OR 1.26 [1.07–1.47]) but near-null vs ATG, consistent with a T-cell depletion depth gradient. BK virus (C1: OR 2.48) and BSI (C1: OR 1.87) were elevated regardless of comparator. Despite elevated infection rates, infection-related mortality was not increased (C1: OR 1.35, Bayesian–frequentist discordant; C2: OR 0.9). Within-PTCy regimen variants (C3) showed no significant differences.

Conclusions. PTCy provides consistent survival, GVHD, and anti-relapse advantages regardless of comparator. Chronic GVHD reduction is the largest treatment effect (OR 0.33). Infection rates are elevated but infection-related mortality is not clearly increased, suggesting the infectious cost is manageable. GRADE certainty is LOW for OS, aGVHD, and RRM; VERY LOW for other outcomes.

Key Findings

1. Chronic GVHD — the largest treatment effect. PTCy reduced moderate-severe chronic GVHD by two-thirds vs CNI+MTX (C1 OR 0.33 [95% CrI 0.29–0.36], k = 21). This is the largest treatment effect in the analysis — substantially exceeding acute GVHD (OR 0.67) — with direct implications for long-term quality of life and immunosuppression duration. The effect attenuates vs ATG (C2 OR 0.79), consistent with ATG also providing cGVHD prophylaxis.

2. Survival and acute GVHD benefit. PTCy was associated with reduced overall mortality (C1 OR 0.79; C2 OR 0.82) and acute GVHD grade II–IV (C1 OR 0.67; C2 OR 0.58) in both comparisons. Effects were consistent across comparators.

3. Anti-relapse benefit preserved. RRM was reduced in both comparisons (C1 OR 0.83; C2 OR 0.79) with no steroid mediation, supporting preserved graft-versus-leukaemia activity.

4. Infections increased but infection-related mortality not clearly elevated. CMV, BSI, and BK virus rates were elevated vs CNI+MTX. However, infection-related mortality was not clearly increased (C1 Bayesian OR 1.35 [Bayesian–frequentist discordant]; C2 OR 0.9). The infectious morbidity appears manageable and does not translate proportionally into death.

5. CMV depletion gradient. PTCy increased CMV vs CNI+MTX (OR 1.26) but was near-null vs ATG (OR 0.98), supporting a T-cell depletion depth gradient (CNI < PTCy ≈ ATG) rather than a PTCy-specific effect.

6. Within-PTCy variants equivalent. C3 found no significant differences across PTCy regimen variants for any outcome, suggesting the PTCy component dominates the regimen effect.

Mechanistic Framework

The findings resolve into a framework built on three separable consequences of PTCy:

  1. GVHD suppression pathway (intended): PTCy eliminates alloreactive T cells → reduced aGVHD (OR 0.67) and profoundly reduced cGVHD (OR 0.33) → less steroid exposure → lower NRM → improved OS. Chronic GVHD is the most affected outcome, consistent with PTCy’s early alloreactive depletion having an outsized impact on chronic alloimmune processes.

  2. GVL preservation pathway (intended): PTCy → reduced relapse-related mortality (C1 OR 0.84; C2 OR 0.79), steroid-independent. This explains the residual OS advantage after GVHD/steroid adjustment.

  3. T-cell lymphodepletion pathway (unintended): PTCy depletes pathogen-specific T cells during engraftment → elevated CMV, BSI, and BK virus. The CMV depletion gradient (CNI < PTCy ≈ ATG) confirms this is a relative depth-of-depletion effect. BK virus has an additional urothelial toxicity component. Critically, the elevated infection rates do not translate clearly into elevated infection-related mortality, suggesting the infectious cost is clinically manageable.

## Abstract

::: {.callout-note appearance="minimal"}

**Background.** Post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy) has become widely adopted for GVHD prophylaxis in allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation, yet comprehensive quantitative evidence synthesis across survival, GVHD, and infection outcomes — and across comparator regimens — has been lacking.

**Methods.** We conducted a Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of `r n_studies` studies (`r n_rcts` RCTs) comparing PTCy to (1) CNI+MTX/MMF-based regimens (Comparison 1), (2) ATG-based regimens (Comparison 2), and (3) within-PTCy regimen variants (Comparison 3). Ten outcomes were analysed: OS, NRM, relapse-related mortality (RRM), acute GVHD grade II–IV, chronic GVHD moderate-severe (NIH), CMV reactivation, BK virus, BSI, IFI, and infection-related mortality (IRM). Steroid-mediation models (M2) tested whether GVHD-driven steroid exposure confounded observed effects.

**Results.** PTCy was associated with improved OS (C1: OR `r fmt_or(or_os$or, or_os$lo, or_os$hi)`; C2: OR `r fmt_or(or_c2_os$or, or_c2_os$lo, or_c2_os$hi)`), reduced aGVHD (C1: OR `r round(or_agvhd$or, 2)`; C2: OR `r round(or_c2_agvhd$or, 2)`), and a substantial reduction in moderate-severe chronic GVHD (C1: OR `r round(or_cgvhd$or, 2)` — the largest treatment effect). RRM was reduced in both comparisons (C1: OR `r round(or_rrm$or, 2)`; C2: OR `r round(or_c2_rrm$or, 2)`), supporting preserved GVL activity. CMV reactivation was increased vs CNI+MTX/MMF (OR `r fmt_or(or_cmv$or, or_cmv$lo, or_cmv$hi)`) but near-null vs ATG, consistent with a T-cell depletion depth gradient. BK virus (C1: OR `r round(or_bk$or, 2)`) and BSI (C1: OR `r round(or_bsi$or, 2)`) were elevated regardless of comparator. Despite elevated infection rates, infection-related mortality was not increased (C1: OR `r round(or_irm$or, 2)`, Bayesian–frequentist discordant; C2: OR `r round(or_c2_irm$or, 2)`). Within-PTCy regimen variants (C3) showed no significant differences.

**Conclusions.** PTCy provides consistent survival, GVHD, and anti-relapse advantages regardless of comparator. Chronic GVHD reduction is the largest treatment effect (OR 0.33). Infection rates are elevated but infection-related mortality is not clearly increased, suggesting the infectious cost is manageable. GRADE certainty is LOW for OS, aGVHD, and RRM; VERY LOW for other outcomes.

### Key Findings

::: {.key-finding}
**1. Chronic GVHD — the largest treatment effect.** PTCy reduced moderate-severe chronic GVHD by two-thirds vs CNI+MTX (C1 OR `r round(or_cgvhd$or, 2)` [95% CrI `r round(or_cgvhd$lo, 2)`–`r round(or_cgvhd$hi, 2)`], k = 21). This is the largest treatment effect in the analysis — substantially exceeding acute GVHD (OR `r round(or_agvhd$or, 2)`) — with direct implications for long-term quality of life and immunosuppression duration. The effect attenuates vs ATG (C2 OR `r round(or_c2_cgvhd$or, 2)`), consistent with ATG also providing cGVHD prophylaxis.
:::

::: {.key-finding}
**2. Survival and acute GVHD benefit.** PTCy was associated with reduced overall mortality (C1 OR `r round(or_os$or, 2)`; C2 OR `r round(or_c2_os$or, 2)`) and acute GVHD grade II–IV (C1 OR `r round(or_agvhd$or, 2)`; C2 OR `r round(or_c2_agvhd$or, 2)`) in both comparisons. Effects were consistent across comparators.
:::

::: {.key-finding}
**3. Anti-relapse benefit preserved.** RRM was reduced in both comparisons (C1 OR `r round(or_rrm$or, 2)`; C2 OR `r round(or_c2_rrm$or, 2)`) with no steroid mediation, supporting preserved graft-versus-leukaemia activity.
:::

::: {.key-finding}
**4. Infections increased but infection-related mortality not clearly elevated.** CMV, BSI, and BK virus rates were elevated vs CNI+MTX. However, infection-related mortality was not clearly increased (C1 Bayesian OR `r round(or_irm$or, 2)` [Bayesian–frequentist discordant]; C2 OR `r round(or_c2_irm$or, 2)`). The infectious morbidity appears manageable and does not translate proportionally into death.
:::

::: {.key-finding}
**5. CMV depletion gradient.** PTCy increased CMV vs CNI+MTX (OR `r round(or_cmv$or, 2)`) but was near-null vs ATG (OR `r round(or_c2_cmv$or, 2)`), supporting a T-cell depletion depth gradient (CNI < PTCy ≈ ATG) rather than a PTCy-specific effect.
:::

::: {.key-finding}
**6. Within-PTCy variants equivalent.** C3 found no significant differences across PTCy regimen variants for any outcome, suggesting the PTCy component dominates the regimen effect.
:::

### Mechanistic Framework

The findings resolve into a framework built on three separable consequences of PTCy:

1. **GVHD suppression pathway** (intended): PTCy eliminates alloreactive T cells → reduced aGVHD (OR 0.67) and profoundly reduced cGVHD (OR 0.33) → less steroid exposure → lower NRM → improved OS. Chronic GVHD is the most affected outcome, consistent with PTCy's early alloreactive depletion having an outsized impact on chronic alloimmune processes.

2. **GVL preservation pathway** (intended): PTCy → reduced relapse-related mortality (C1 OR 0.84; C2 OR 0.79), steroid-independent. This explains the residual OS advantage after GVHD/steroid adjustment.

3. **T-cell lymphodepletion pathway** (unintended): PTCy depletes pathogen-specific T cells during engraftment → elevated CMV, BSI, and BK virus. The CMV depletion gradient (CNI < PTCy ≈ ATG) confirms this is a relative depth-of-depletion effect. BK virus has an additional urothelial toxicity component. Critically, the elevated infection *rates* do not translate clearly into elevated infection-related *mortality*, suggesting the infectious cost is clinically manageable.
